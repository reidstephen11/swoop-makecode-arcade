SWOOP — run sheet

Year 6 STEM Launchpad taster · MakeCode Arcade, blocks · 50 minutes · ~22 students, one laptop each

Before they arrive

  • SWOOP preloaded on every laptop, and confirmed to open straight into the Blocks view — no URL typing, no import, no sign-in.
  • Mission cards cut (one A4 sheet makes two cards). Missions 1–5 face down in a pile per bench; the STUCK card stays face up next to each laptop all session.
  • Play it yourself once so you know what "too slow" feels like before they tell you.

What each mission actually does — measured, not estimated

#The editWhat changes when it works
—Starter, untouchedA passive player loses their first life at 5–12 s and all three by 25–36 s. Competent play for 60 s scores about 20 — playable, but visibly slow.
1Redraw the bucket; change the skyCosmetic only. Nothing can break. The game still runs on a white sky — the crow keeps a black outline — but dark skies look far better.
2dropEvery 2500 → ~700, fallSpeed 15 → ~60Score over 60 s goes from ~20 to ~54 — roughly 2.5× the catch rate. Felt immediately, which is the point.
3Add on score 15 → game over WINReaching 15 ends the game with "YOU WIN!". Verified on screen.
4Duplicate the crow stack, retime it to 2000 msScore 42 in 60 s with 2 lives left — harder, still winnable. With Mission 3 also applied, a good player wins in about 16 s.
5Sound on catch, bigger bucket, countdown, fifth loot itemAll four compile and run.

The questions you will get

"My crow flies backwards."
They've sent a crow in from the right. The sprite only faces one way, so it moon-walks. It works — treat it as a good bug, not a fault. Fixing it properly means flipping every frame in the crow's animation, which is a real Mission 5 job.

"I set dropEvery to 0 and it went mad."
About 95 sprites on screen, score rockets, no crash and no hang — tested. Let it happen, then ask them what number would be silly-but-playable.

"Where do I draw the bucket?"
Click the picture inside the block, not the block. The drawing grid opens over the whole screen; the green Done button is bottom right.

"Nothing happened."
Nine times in ten the block is floating, not clicked on. Second most likely: they changed a number but didn't press ▶ again.

"Can I keep it?"
Share → email themselves the link. See the caveat below.

Known limits — read before the session

The school filter is untested. Three things have not been checked on a school laptop: that arcade.makecode.com loads at all, that the preloaded project opens, and that a student can create a share link. The third one decides whether the take-home works — worth testing on one laptop before the day rather than in front of 22 students.

No public share link has been published yet, so there is currently no URL to fall back on if a laptop hasn't been preloaded.

  • No music, by design. Twenty-two laptops of chiptune is a wall of noise. Sound is a Mission 5 side quest, not a default — if it takes off, be ready to ask for headphones or a volume-down.
  • Missions 1–3 are the guaranteed core. A student who finishes 3 has a complete, winnable game. Mission 4 is where most will end; Mission 5 is stretch and is deliberately unspecified.
  • Nothing a student does today can break the starter beyond an undo. Every sloppy edit worth worrying about was tested: zero spawn interval, absurd fall speed, white sky. None crash.
